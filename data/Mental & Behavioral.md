Source Standard: WHO / CDC / ICD-10 Clinical Reference Data
Document Purpose: Baseline clinical context for Healthcare AI Assistant symptom matching.

| Disease / Condition | ICD-10 Code | Primary / Common Symptoms | Secondary / Severe Symptoms | Risk Factors & Trigger Context | Urgent Warning Signs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) | F32.9 | Persistent feelings of sadness, emptiness, or hopelessness; loss of interest in activities (anhedonia); fatigue. | Significant weight/appetite changes, insomnia or hypersomnia, feelings of worthlessness, trouble concentrating. | Family history of depression, major life stressors, trauma, chronic illness, hormonal shifts. | Expressing suicidal ideation, making suicide plans, active self-harm behaviors, total withdrawal from reality. |
| Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) | F41.1 | Excessive, uncontrollable worry about everyday things; persistent muscle tension; irritability; feeling restless or on edge. | Sleep disturbances (difficulty falling/staying asleep), chronic fatigue, difficulty concentrating, headaches. | Genetics, chronic long-term stress, history of trauma or abuse, female sex, personality traits (inhibited/negative). | Panic attacks with chest pain/shortness of breath, severe dissociation, complete functional incapacitation. |
| Panic Disorder | F41.0 | Recurrent, unexpected panic attacks; intense sudden fear; physical symptoms like racing heart, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath. | Persistent fear of having another panic attack, avoidance of places/situations associated with panic (agoraphobia). | Family history of panic attacks, major life stress or trauma, history of physical or sexual abuse in childhood. | Severe chest pain mimicking a heart attack, uncontrollable hyperventilation, sudden feeling of choking or impending doom. |
| Bipolar Disorder | F31.9 | Alternating episodes of emotional highs (mania/hypomania) and lows (depression); mood swings; changes in energy and activity levels. | During manic phase: grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, rapid speech, reckless behavior. During depressive phase: severe fatigue, hopelessness. | Strong genetic predisposition, chemical imbalances in the brain, extreme high-stress environments, substance abuse. | Psychotic features (delusions/hallucinations during mania), extreme risk-taking behavior, acute suicidal crisis. |
| Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) | F43.10 | Intrusive memories or flashbacks of a traumatic event; recurring nightmares; severe emotional distress or physical reactions to triggers. | Avoidance of trauma-related reminders, negative changes in beliefs/mood, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response. | Exposure to life-threatening trauma (combat, assault, accidents), lack of social support, pre-existing anxiety/depression. | Severe self-destructive behavior, aggressive outbursts, profound dissociation, acute suicidal ideation. |
| Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) | F42.9 | Obsessions (recurrent unwanted thoughts, urges, or mental images causing anxiety); compulsions (repetitive behaviors performed to reduce anxiety). | Spending excessive time daily on rituals, distress when rituals cannot be performed, avoidance of trigger situations. | Family history, brain structure differences, stressful life events, pediatric autoimmune neuropsychiatric disorders (PANDAS). | Complete inability to function or leave home due to paralyzing rituals, severe self-harm from physical compulsions. |
| Schizophrenia | F20.9 | Hallucinations (hearing voices, seeing things), delusions (fixed false beliefs), disorganized speech or thinking, flat affect. | Lack of drive or motivation (avolition), social withdrawal, cognitive deficits, catatonia or erratic movement. | Genetics (family history), structural brain abnormalities, prenatal exposure to toxins/viruses, severe psychological trauma. | Command hallucinations directing self-harm or harm to others, acute catatonia, violent outbursts, total detachment from reality. |
| Attention-Deficit / Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) | F90.9 | Inattention (difficulty sustaining focus, losing items, forgetfulness) and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity (fidgeting, talking excessively, interrupting). | Poor time management, chronic disorganization, mood swings, low frustration tolerance, academic/work underachievement. | Genetics, premature birth, low birth weight, prenatal exposure to alcohol/tobacco, environmental toxin exposures. | Severe depression or anxiety secondary to chronic failure, dangerous impulsive risk-taking leading to physical injury. |
